## Key timeline (late 2024 – early 2026)

- **Jun 2024** — RIAA sues Suno and Udio on behalf of UMG/Sony/Warner
- **Oct 2025** — UMG settles with Udio, enters licensing agreement
- **Nov 2025** — Warner settles with Suno, announces licensed 2026 platform
- **Jan 2026** — ACE-Step v1.5 released (ACE Studio + StepFun, China)
- **Feb 18 2026** — Google launches Lyria 3 in Gemini app
- **Feb 24 2026** — Google acquires ProducerAI (formerly Riffusion)
- **Ongoing** — Sony/UMG v. Suno continues; independent artist class actions pending

## The core tension

Commercial AI music companies (Suno, Udio) trained on copyrighted recordings without licensing, arguing fair use. The music industry disagrees. Meanwhile:

- **FOSS models** (ACE-Step v1.5, Stable Audio Open) use legally licensed or royalty-free training data — they sidestep the legal problem entirely
- **Chinese models** operate largely outside Western IP enforcement jurisdiction  
- **Google** is playing both sides: licensing deals with rights holders, while quietly absorbing the talent that built the disruptive tools

The FOSS/Chinese combination is the long-term structural threat to both the commercial AI music companies AND the labels — a locally-runnable, MIT-licensed model that sounds as good as Suno v4.5 cannot be sued into oblivion.
