# Japanese Honkaku: The Art of the Orthodox Mystery

## ⛩️ Defining Honkaku (本格)
**Honkaku**—literally "orthodox" or "proper"—refers to a subgenre of detective fiction that prioritizes the purely intellectual challenge of solving a crime. It is a strictly **Materialist and Reductionist** approach to mystery, where atmospheric elements serve as a framework for a complex logical puzzle (the "Whodunit" or "Howdunit").

### The Honkaku Creed
1. **Fair Play**: Every clue required to solve the mystery must be presented to the reader.
2. **Logical Rigor**: The solution cannot rely on coincidence, supernatural interference, or hidden information.
3. **The Game**: The author and reader are engaged in a peer-to-peer technical competition.

## 📜 The Historical Pillars

### 1. Edogawa Rampo (江戸川 乱歩)
The father of Japanese mystery fiction, whose name is a phonetic rendering of Edgar Allan Poe. While his earlier work leaned into the *ero-guro-nansensu* (erotic-grotesque-nonsense) style, he laid the foundations for the logical detective story in Japan.
- **Key Detective**: Kogoro Akechi.

### 2. Seishi Yokomizo (横溝 正史)
The master of the rural gothic. Yokomizo combined the "Locked Room" influence of John Dickson Carr with Japanese feudal aesthetics and post-war societal trauma.
- **The Inugami Clan (犬神家の一族)**: A masterpiece of inherited bitterness and complex geometric murder patterns.
- **Key Detective**: Kosuke Kindaichi (the disheveled, eccentric genius).

## 🚀 The Shin-Honkaku (New Orthodox) Movement
In the late1980s, a new generation of writers led by **Soji Shimada** rejected the "Social School" of Realism (socialist-critique mysteries) in favor of returning to the pure logic of the Golden Age.

### 1. Soji Shimada (島田 荘司)
The "God of Shin-Honkaku." His debut novel, *The Tokyo Zodiac Murders*, remains one of the most mechanically complex mysteries ever written.
- **The Tokyo Zodiac Murders**: Utilizing the "Vibearchitecting" of a fictional necro-sculpture to hide a materialist reality.
- **Key Detective**: Kiyoshi Mitarai.

### 2. Yukito Ayatsuji (綾辻 行人)
A pivotal figure in the movement, known for his "Mansion" series, which utilizes architectural anomalies as the primary logic gate of the mystery.
- **The Decagon House Murders**: A direct homage to Agatha Christie's *And Then There Were None*, but with a sharper focus on the mechanics of the crime.
