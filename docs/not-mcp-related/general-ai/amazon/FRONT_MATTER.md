## A Note on How This Book Was Made

**Let's be honest: most of this book was written by AI.**

In November 2025, I opened Google's Antigravity IDE (free tier, bless those
VC subsidies), connected it to Gemini 3 Pro with web search, and asked it
to write comprehensive documentation about the current AI landscape.

It did.

Then I opened Cursor IDE, connected Claude Opus 4.5, and asked it to make
the prose less robotic, add depth to the sci-fi analysis, and fact-check
the technical details.

It did that too.

My contribution? Curation. Direction. Knowing which questions to ask.
Catching the hallucinations (there were several). Adding the snark. Making
sure TRUMPF got its due credit for not price-gouging the world.

**The result is what you're holding:** A book about AI, written by AI,
edited by AI, sold on a platform whose recommendations are powered by AI,
that you probably found through an AI-assisted search.

**Is this the future of publishing?** Maybe. Probably. The economics are
hard to argue with: this book cost approximately $0 in generation costs
and about six hours of human time.

**Is it any good?** That's for you to decide. The research is solid (web
search doesn't hallucinate URLs). The prose is readable (Claude has taste).
The analysis is... well, it's what a very well-read AI thinks about the
state of AI, which is either profound or recursive depending on your
philosophical commitments.

**At $2.50, you're not being ripped off.** The information density per
dollar is probably higher than most business books that charge $29.99 for
200 pages of padding around one good idea.

Consider this an experiment in AI-human collaboration—and a snapshot of a
moment when the tools to write a book became accessible to anyone with
curiosity and an internet connection.

Enjoy the future. It wrote itself.

—Sandra, November 2025
