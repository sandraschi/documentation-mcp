# Afterword

## Where We Are, Where We're Going

You've just read approximately 50,000 words about artificial intelligence,
written mostly by artificial intelligence, in November 2025.

If that sentence doesn't make you pause, it should.

**Three years ago,** ChatGPT didn't exist. GitHub Copilot was a curiosity.
"AI" meant Alexa mishearing your music requests. The idea that a machine
could write coherent prose about the state of its own field would have
seemed like science fiction.

**Today,** AI writes books, codes applications, generates videos, designs
chips, and is beginning to conduct scientific research. The pace of change
has been so rapid that this book will be partially outdated by the time
you finish reading it.

**What comes next?** Nobody knows. The experts disagree violently. The
doomers predict extinction; the accelerationists predict utopia; the
exhausted middle just wants the discourse to calm down.

This book doesn't take a strong position on where we're headed because
honest uncertainty is more valuable than confident prediction. What it
does offer is a snapshot—a detailed, researched, reasonably accurate
picture of where we are right now.

**The hardware:** Nvidia's accidental monopoly, TSMC's irreplaceable fabs,
Zeiss's impossible mirrors, TRUMPF's reasonably-priced lasers. The physical
infrastructure that makes AI possible is concentrated in ways that should
terrify policymakers.

**The software:** GPT, Claude, Gemini—models so capable they're changing
how we work, think, and create. Agentic systems that don't just answer
questions but pursue goals. Open-source alternatives democratizing access.

**The geopolitics:** A world splitting into Western and Chinese AI
ecosystems, with export controls trying to slow a technology that moves
faster than policy. Taiwan at the center of everything.

**The philosophy:** Old debates rendered moot by engineering progress. New
questions about consciousness, creativity, and what it means to be human
in a world where machines can do most cognitive tasks.

**The culture:** Doomers vs. accelerationists, safety vs. capabilities,
fear vs. excitement. A discourse so polarized that nuance becomes a
radical position.

**The science fiction:** The prophets who saw it coming. Gibson's
cyberspace, Stephenson's metaverse, Vinge's Blight, Lem's incomprehensible
ocean. These aren't just stories—they're the conceptual vocabulary we use
to think about AI.
