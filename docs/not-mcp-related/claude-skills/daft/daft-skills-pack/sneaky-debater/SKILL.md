# Sneaky Debater

Master catalog of rhetorical tricks and logical fallacies - learn them to DEFEND against them, not to use them!

## Overview

This skill catalogs every dirty trick used in dishonest debates. Educational purpose: recognize manipulation and argue honestly.

## The Classic Fallacies

### Ad Hominem
**Attack person, not argument**:
- "You're just saying that because you're [identity]"
- "Of course YOU would think that"

**Why it works**: Discredits speaker, avoids addressing argument  
**Defense**: "Address my argument, not my characteristics"

### Strawman
**Misrepresent to defeat easily**:
- Original: "We should reduce pollution"
- Strawman: "You want to ban all cars and live in caves!"

**Why it works**: Easier to defeat fake argument than real one  
**Defense**: "That's not my position. Address what I actually said"

### False Dilemma
**Only two options presented**:
- "Either you support this war or you hate our country"
- Ignores: oppose war AND love country

**Why it works**: Forces choice between extremes  
**Defense**: "Those aren't the only options. Here's a third..."

### Appeal to Authority (Misused)
**Authority outside expertise**:
- "Einstein believed in God, so religion is true"
- Einstein = physicist, not theologian

**Why it works**: Name-dropping impressive person  
**Defense**: "What's the evidence, regardless of who said it?"

### Appeal to Nature
**Natural = good fallacy**:
- "It's natural, therefore it's healthy"
- Arsenic is natural; antibiotics aren't

**Why it works**: "Natural" has positive connotations  
**Defense**: "Natural doesn't mean good or safe"

### Slippery Slope
**Exaggerate consequences**:
- "If we allow gay marriage, next people will marry dogs!"
- No evidence for the slope

**Why it works**: Fear of worst-case scenario  
**Defense**: "Show evidence that slope exists"

## Advanced Manipulation

### Gish Gallop
**Rapid-fire false claims**:
- List 20 misleading points quickly
- Takes 1 minute to say, hours to debunk
- Audience sees unrebutted claims as conceded

**Why it works**: Asymmetry of bullshit  
**Defense**: "Pick your strongest claim, we'll examine that thoroughly"

### Motte and Bailey
**Defend weak claim by retreating to strong one**:
- Bailey (weak): "All police are bastards"
- Motte (strong): "Some police behave badly"
- When challenged, retreat to motte
- When unchallenged, advance bailey

**Why it works**: Appears reasonable while pushing extreme view  
**Defense**: "That's not what you said originally. Defend your actual claim"

### Moving the Goalposts
**Change requirements after they're met**:
- "Show me evidence!" → shows evidence
- "That's not enough evidence!" → shows more
- "Well that evidence doesn't count!" → infinite regression

**Why it works**: Never have to admit being wrong  
**Defense**: "We agreed on the standard. It's met. Accept it or explain why you're changing standards"

### Sealioning
**Exhaust through fake politeness**:
- "I'm just asking questions!"
- Endless requests for evidence
- Never satisfied, infinite demands
- Appear reasonable while wasting time

**Why it works**: Looks like genuine inquiry  
**Defense**: "I've answered. If genuinely interested, read [source]"

### The Kafka Trap
**Denial proves guilt**:
- "You're racist!"
- "No I'm not"
- "See? Denying it proves you are!"

**Why it works**: Unfalsifiable accusation  
**Defense**: "That's a logical trap. Denial isn't evidence"

## Sneaky Tactics

### Anchoring
**Extreme to make moderate seem reasonable**:
- Start: "We should deport EVERYONE!"
- "Compromise": "Okay just deport half"
- Made extreme position seem moderate

### Foot in the Door
**Small concession → big claim**:
- "You agree free speech has some limits?" (yes)
- "So you agree THIS speech should be banned!" (doesn't follow)

### False Equivalence
**Treat unequal things as equal**:
- "Both sides have problems"
- One side: mean tweets
- Other side: violence
- Implies equivalence where none exists

### Whataboutism (Tu Quoque)
**Deflect with hypocrisy**:
- "You say pollution is bad but you drive a car!"
- Doesn't address whether pollution is bad
- Even hypocrites can be right about facts

### Tone Policing
**Dismiss based on delivery**:
- "I can't take you seriously when you're so emotional"
- Avoids addressing substance
- Dismisses based on affect, not logic

## Pattern Recognition

### Shifting Burden of Proof
**Make claim, demand opponent disprove**:
- "Prove God DOESN'T exist!"
- Impossible to prove negative
- Burden is on claimant

**Honest debate**: Provide evidence for positive claims

### Cherry Picking
**Select only supporting evidence**:
- Cite 3 studies supporting position
- Ignore 97 studies contradicting it
- Mislead through selective presentation

**Honest debate**: Address strongest contrary evidence

### Moving to Meta
**When losing argument, argue about arguing**:
- Original topic: Climate change
- Losing: Switch to "Why are you so hostile?"
- Or: "This shows the problem with debate culture"
- Avoids admitting being wrong

**Defense**: "That's a meta-discussion. Let's finish the object-level first"

## Honest Debate Principles

**Do**:
- Steel-man opponent (argue against strongest version)
- Admit when wrong
- Provide evidence for claims
- Stick to the point
- Ask clarifying questions genuinely

**Don't**:
- Use these tricks!
- Attack person vs ideas
- Misrepresent positions
- Move goalposts
- Ignore evidence

## The Meta-Lesson

**Knowing these tricks makes you**:
- Better arguer (avoid them)
- Better thinker (spot them)
- Better communicator (value honesty)
- More persuasive (honest arguments win long-term)

**Using them makes you**:
- Win arguments dishonestly
- Lose credibility long-term
- Undermine productive discourse

## Detection and Response

When someone uses these tricks:
1. Name the fallacy calmly
2. Request they address actual argument
3. If they persist → bad faith confirmed
4. Disengage or switch tactics

**Sometimes**: Best response is no response (don't feed trolls)
