# Accelerationist

Educational guide to accelerationist ideology - why "make things worse to spark revolution" is terrible strategy.

## Overview

This skill teaches critical analysis of accelerationist thinking - the idea that making conditions worse will force revolutionary change. Educational focus on why this logic fails and causes real harm.

## What Is Accelerationism?

**The core claim**:
- Current system is irredeemably broken
- Reform is impossible or insufficient
- Therefore: Make things WORSE to provoke collapse
- After collapse: Revolution/better system emerges

**Versions**:
- Left accelerationism: "Accelerate capitalism's contradictions"
- Right accelerationism: "Accelerate diversity/immigration to provoke backlash"
- Tech accelerationism: "Move fast and break things (including society)"

## Why This Logic Fails

### Problem 1: Assumes Controlled Collapse

**The fantasy**:
- Things get worse → controlled collapse
- Revolution happens → good people win
- Better system emerges → everyone benefits

**The reality**:
- Collapses are chaotic, not controlled
- Worst people often seize power in chaos
- Vulnerable people suffer most during collapse
- No guarantee better system emerges

**Historical examples**: Most revolutions don't go as planned

### Problem 2: Real People Suffer

**Accelerationist**: "Make things worse!"  
**Result**: Real people suffer in the meantime  
**Who suffers most**: Vulnerable populations

**The privilege**: Only people insulated from consequences can advocate making things worse

### Problem 3: False Dilemma

**Claimed options**:
- Reform (insufficient)
- Accelerate collapse (only real solution)

**Ignored option**:
- Incremental improvement
- Harm reduction
- Building alternatives
- Actual organizing

**The false binary**: Reform or revolution (why not both?)

### Problem 4: Gambling Other People's Lives

**The gamble**:
- Make things worse (certain harm)
- Hope for revolution (uncertain)
- Hope revolution succeeds (more uncertain)
- Hope better system emerges (most uncertain)

**Probability**: Real harm now for uncertain benefit later

**Who bears cost**: Not the accelerationist (usually privileged)

## Recognizing Accelerationist Logic

### Red Flags

- "Things have to get worse before they get better"
- "Reform is useless, only revolution works"
- "Support the worst candidates to provoke backlash"
- "Let the system collapse, we'll build better"
- "Suffering now will lead to liberation later"

### The Pattern

**Step 1**: Identify real problem (often correct!)  
**Step 2**: Declare reform impossible (debatable)  
**Step 3**: Advocate making things worse (where it goes wrong)  
**Step 4**: Promise revolution will fix it (unjustified leap)

## Variations

### Left Accelerationism
"Accelerate capitalism's contradictions!"
- Make conditions worse for workers
- Hope this provokes revolution
- Result: Real workers suffer now for theoretical future benefit

### Right Accelerationism
"Accelerate diversity/immigration to provoke backlash!"
- Make demographic change faster
- Hope this provokes nationalist reaction
- Result: Real people suffer for political strategy

### Tech Accelerationism  
"Move fast and break things!"
- Deploy technology without safety checks
- "Innovation" over caution
- Result: Real harms (see: social media, gig economy)

## Why People Fall For It

**Accelerationism appeals to**:
- Frustration with slow reform
- Desire for dramatic change
- Revolutionary romanticism
- Certainty that system is irredeemable
- Impatience with incremental progress

**But**: Impatience isn't strategy

## The Honest Critique of Reform

**Reformers may be right**: System has deep problems  
**Accelerationists may be right**: Reform is often insufficient  
**Accelerationists are wrong**: Making things worse isn't the solution

**Better approaches**:
- Build alternative institutions
- Support mutual aid
- Organize communities
- Push for policy changes
- Create parallel systems
- **Don't actively make people's lives worse!**

## The Trolley Problem Version

**Would you**:
- Pull lever to kill 1 person now
- Hope it prevents system that kills 10 later
- No guarantee the 10 will be saved
- Certain harm vs uncertain benefit

**Accelerationism**: Pull the lever (make things worse) based on hope not evidence

## Educational Use

Use this skill to:
- Teach about political extremism
- Examine revolutionary ideologies critically
- Understand why "worse is better" fails
- Recognize when frustration becomes recklessness
- Promote evidence-based activism

## The Alternative

**Instead of accelerating collapse**:
- Harm reduction (help people now)
- Building alternatives (create better options)
- Organizing (build power)
- Incremental wins (accumulate victories)
- Mutual aid (support communities)

**These actually help people** vs gambling on theoretical revolution

## The Questions to Ask

**For any accelerationist claim**:
1. Who suffers when things get worse?
2. What evidence suggests revolution will succeed?
3. What makes you think better system emerges?
4. Why not build alternatives while reducing harm?
5. Are you willing to experience the suffering you're accelerating?

**Usually**: Answers reveal privilege and unjustified optimism

## The Honest Position

**Systems have problems** (often true)  
**Reform is hard** (true)  
**Making things worse helps** (false!)  
**Revolution guaranteed to succeed** (false!)  
**Better system guaranteed** (false!)

**Better**: Fight for improvements while building alternatives
