# Ming maritime collapse — Knowledge brief + footgun verdict

**Status:** MCD writing brief · **Charter:** Factual timeline is Knowledge voice; **footgun ranking** is founder verdict (see [FOUNDER_PERSPECTIVES.md](./FOUNDER_PERSPECTIVES.md)).

**Do not** open the Ming article with mushy bothsidesism ("historians argue whether isolation happened") — that reads like **nationalist revisionist whitewash** to readers who know the fleet was scrapped and ocean policy reversed.

---

## What to document straight (sourced, dated)

| Period | What happened (lead with this) |
|--------|------------------------------|
| **1405–1433** | Zheng He treasure voyages — largest maritime expeditionary program of the age; Indian Ocean trade, tribute, projection. |
| **1433** | Last voyage ends; **no successor program**. Zheng He dies (1433/1435); he was **lucky not to be executed** — court factions already hostile to eunuch-admirals and maritime spending (founder emphasis: survival was political, not merit). |
| **Post-1433** | Pivot to northern defense, tribute inward, **destruction and neglect of the fleet** — ships allowed to rot; **burning/dismantling** of capability (document what sources say: e.g. 1477 destruction of Zheng He's logs in Nanjing in some accounts; **1500** ban on building **two-masted** ocean-going ships in Ming law — verify citations in draft). |
| **Long run** | Maritime trade continues via smugglers and regional actors, but **state abdication** of the oceanic lead Europe then filled — Portuguese, then others. |

Regional exceptions and silver flows do **not** cancel the footgun — they are footnotes to **state choice**.

---

## Founder verdict (labeled, may appear in "Why this matters" box)

In the history of **footguns**, the Ming decision to **throw away** the treasure-fleet world after Zheng He is a candidate for **the most horrible** — a self-inflicted amputation of trade, cultural exchange, and strategic optionality ~500 years before the modern PRC English/script debates.

Not "China was always closed." **This dynasty, this turn**, this waste of a lead.

## Voice rules for the HTML page

1. **Lead with policy and dates**, not historiographical fog.  
2. Cite Ming shi / reputable secondary (Dreyer, Levathes, etc.) — not CCP triumph narratives, not sinophobe memes.  
3. **Forbidden framing:** "Historians argue the isolation narrative is exaggerated" as the **first** thing a learner reads — that is the whitewash tone we reject.  
4. **Allowed:** Scholarly detail disputes (extent of ship burning, local trade volume) **after** the main timeline — never as **Pirie-grade fallacy** opener: "historians argue" false balance belongs in [*Book of the Fallacy*](LOGIC_AND_FALLACIES.md) (Madsen Pirie, Calibre-Bibliothek), not our first paragraph.  
5. Link forward: Qing, Opium Wars, Taiwan strait, modern navy — as **consequences chain**, not destiny.
