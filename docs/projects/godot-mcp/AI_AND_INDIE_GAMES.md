# AI and Indie Games — Is It Over?

**Canonical copy (edit there first):** `D:/Dev/repos/godot-mcp/docs/ai-and-indie-games.md`

Fleet summary: whether AI-assisted game scaffolding (e.g. VibeCode Runner in one session) means indie development is dead.

---

## Short answer

**No.** AI compresses boilerplate and first playable slices — not months of design, polish, marketing, and luck. The floor for “a game exists” dropped; the bar for **standing out** rose.

---

## What AI actually replaced

- Afternoon of scaffold + wiring + export docs  
- Rapid enemy/boss iteration  
- Hobby path: idea → playable → itch.io toy  

## What it did not replace

- Taste, novel mechanics, cohesion, polish, ship & community  
- Commercial indie pipeline (trailer, store, updates, business)  

---

## Before vs now

| Before | Now |
|--------|-----|
| Few finished *any* game | Many finish *a* game |
| Jam-quality stood out | Jam-quality is table stakes |
| Moat = “I shipped” | Moat = voice + craft + people |

---

## Who is hurt / helped

| | |
|--|--|
| **Weaker** | Lazy clones, labor-only differentiation |
| **Alive** | Indie as category — harder middle, not extinct |
| **Better** | Hobby “little games” — faster, lower sunk cost |
| **Unchanged** | Hits still need ideas, craft, timing |

Same pattern as Unity, asset stores, and free itch uploads: **more noise, not fewer artists.**

---

## godot-mcp stance

For hobby makers **not** pursuing indie careers: AI + `just ship` makes toys faster — you are not competing with *Brotato* on Steam.

For commercial indie: AI is a multiplier; assume genre clones are cheap — invest in **why your game exists**.
