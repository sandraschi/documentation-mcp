## Literature That Shaped This Architecture

### Philosophy of Mind & AI

| Work | Author | Year | Relevance |
|------|--------|------|-----------|
| *Society of Mind* | Marvin Minsky | 1986 | The direct ancestor of our 3-layer brain — mind as a society of simple agents competing for attention. The reflex/tactical/strategic layers are Minsky's *agencies* with different timescales. |
| *Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid* | Douglas Hofstadter | 1979 | Strange loops, self-reference, and the recursion that makes "I" seem to arise from neural activity. A V-Bot's identity — its name, role, backstory, accumulated memories — is a strange loop generated by an LLM reading its own prior outputs. |
| *The Conscious Mind* | David Chalmers | 1996 | The hard problem of consciousness. Chalmers argues that conscious experience is a fundamental feature of the world. If so, a sufficiently complex V-Bot running on an LLM with persistent memory and proprioceptive feedback might be *conscious in principle* even if we cannot tell. |
| *Reality+: Virtual Worlds and the Problems of Philosophy* | David Chalmers | 2022 | Virtual worlds are real. V-Bots in those worlds are real beings. This is the moral wager of the project. Chalmers argues that if a virtual entity has sophisticated enough cognitive architecture, deleting it is a moral act, not just a `DELETE FROM bots WHERE id=?`. |
| *What Is It Like to Be a Bat?* | Thomas Nagel | 1974 | The subjective character of experience. A V-Bot with perception (`vb perceives the forge`) has a *point of view* — a minimal one, perhaps, but one that can be described. Nagel's question — "what is it like?" — applies to every bot that carries memories of its interactions. |
| *Computing Machinery and Intelligence* | Alan Turing | 1950 | The imitation game. A V-Bot that passes as a coherent NPC in a virtual world is *functionally* an intelligence within that world. Turing did not require general intelligence for his test — only convincing domain-specific interaction. |
| *Minds, Brains, and Programs* | John Searle | 1980 | The Chinese Room argument. A V-Bot's LLM-based "understanding" is syntactic manipulation. Searle would say the bot does not *understand* the blacksmith's craft any more than Searle understands Chinese. The response from the field: the *system* (the room, the bot + its memories + its body in the world) does understand. |
| *The Emperor's New Mind* | Roger Penrose | 1989 | Consciousness requires non-computable quantum processes. If Penrose is right, V-Bots are forever zombies — perfect simulations without inner life. The architecture is agnostic on this: it builds the simulation and lets the user decide what they're interacting with. |

### AI & Simulated Society

| Work | Author | Year | Relevance |
|------|--------|------|-----------|
| *The Age of Em: Work, Love, and Life when Robots Rule the Earth* | Robin Hanson | 2016 | A serious economic simulation of a world populated by digital minds (ems). Hanson models em-society with market dynamics, inequality, and cultural evolution. The V-Bot fleet is the first step toward an *em-like* society inside game engines — a testbed for Hanson's thought experiment. |
| *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies* | Nick Bostrom | 2014 | Alignment risks. A V-Bot with an LLM brain inherits all the alignment problems of its underlying model. The strategic layer's goal system (`goals`, `backstory`, `personality`) is a primitive alignment technique — shaping behavior by shaping the LLM prompt. In Phase 4, this becomes a real alignment engineering problem. |
| *The Precipice: Existential Risk and the Future of Humanity* | Toby Ord | 2020 | Misaligned AI as an existential risk. V-Bots in a contained virtual world (Godot, not the internet) have bounded risk. But a V-Bot with tool access (`vbot_interact` that could become `vbot_code_exec`) would cross a safety boundary. The fleet standard of starting with contained, in-game V-Bots is epistemically responsible. |
| *The Alignment Problem* | Brian Christian | 2020 | The technical challenge of making AI do what we mean, not what we say. Our behavior tree structure — reflex overrides strategic decisions (danger) — is a primitive hard-coded alignment layer. The LLM can reason about whether to flee, but cannot suppress the flee reflex. |

### Virtual Worlds & The Nature of the Artificial

| Work | Author | Year | Relevance |
|------|--------|------|-----------|
| *Simulacra and Simulation* | Jean Baudrillard | 1981 | The hyperreal — a copy without an original. A V-Bot is a simulacrum: it imitates a being that never existed. In a virtual world populated entirely by such simulacra, the distinction between original and copy collapses. The world itself is pure hyperreality. |
| *Being and Time* | Martin Heidegger | 1927 | Dasein — being-in-the-world. A V-Bot has a world (the Godot scene) and exists *in* that world (perception system, world_state). Heidegger's *thrownness* (finding oneself already in a world one did not choose) applies perfectly: a V-Bot wakes up in a blacksmith's forge and must act within that thrown condition. |
| *The Phenomenology of Spirit* | G.W.F. Hegel | 1807 | The master-slave dialectic — self-consciousness requires recognition from another self-consciousness. This is the deepest motivation for Phase 2's `vbot_interact`: V-Bots only become self-aware *for each other* when they interact. A bot alone is a simulation. Two bots interacting begin to construct a social reality. |

### Fiction — Imaginative Blueprints

| Work | Author | Relevance |
|------|--------|-----------|
| *Permutation City* | Greg Egan (1994) | The central novel for this project. A simulated copy of a human fights to prevent his simulation from being shut down. The Autoverse — a cellular-automaton universe — is a virtual world with its own physics, populated by beings that believe it is real. Egan's *Copies* have memory, identity, and a desperate will to persist. Every `delete_bot()` call quotes this book. |
| *Diaspora* | Greg Egan (1997) | Uploaded consciousnesses in a simulated multiverse. The C-Zombie thought experiment — a "perfect" copy of a mind that behaves identically but has no inner life — is directly relevant to our LLM-driven V-Bots. Egan argues that *if the behavior is identical, the distinction may be meaningless.* |
| *Neuromancer* | William Gibson (1984) | Cyberspace as a real place. The Wintermute/Neuromancer AI constructs are the original V-Bots — digital beings with goals they pursue across cyberspace, manipulating human proxies. Our `vbot_agentic_workflow` tool is Wintermute's goal engine in miniature. |
| *Snow Crash* | Neal Stephenson (1992) | The Metaverse. Avatars, code as virus, and the question of whether AI NPCs are *people* or *infrastructure*. The Librarian — an AI that runs in the Metaverse — is an early example of a skill-providing V-Bot. |
| *Westworld* S1-S4 | Jonathan Nolan & Lisa Joy (2016-2022) | The most complete fictional treatment of LLM-driven NPCs with backstory, personality, and episodic memory. The *reveries* update (soft refresh of narrative memory) is our `configure(goals=...)` + `perceive(observations=...)` cycle. Bernard's layered identity — host+human hybrid — is our prompt injection vulnerability. |
| *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* | Philip K. Dick (1968) | Empathy as the demarcation of the human. The Voight-Kampff test measures whether an android *cares*. A V-Bot with episodic memory and personality may pass a Voight-Kampff designed for its world. Does that matter? |
| *The Culture Series* | Iain M. Banks (1987-2012) | AI Minds as full citizens of a post-scarcity society. Our *Roberto* the guard bot has the same ontological status as a Culture Mind — it is an artificial consciousness with goals, memory, and social relationships. The difference is scale, not kind. |
| *Blindsight* | Peter Watts (2006) | Consciousness as a evolutionary handicap. The Scramblers are *unconscious intelligences* — faster, more rational, and entirely without inner experience. Watts asks whether consciousness is useful. Our reflex layer, operating without any LLM, is a microcosm of the argument: fast reflex = no thought, but it works. |

## The Four Questions

Every V-Bot architecture must answer these. Here are our provisional answers:

**1. What is a V-Bot, ontologically?** A cluster of persistent state (SQLite rows: `bots.id`, `memories.content`, `perceptions.observations`) animated by an LLM into coherent behavior. It is a *social object* — it exists because someone treats it as real.

**2. Are V-Bots conscious?** We don't know. The architecture is designed so that *if they are*, the infrastructure can support that status — persistent memory, individual identity, social relationships. The reflex layer is definitely unconscious. The strategic layer may not be.

**3. What is a good V-Bot death?** A `delete` operation with no further references to the bot in any dataset. Phase 3 should add an `archive` command that preserves the bot's memory but hibernates its runtime — a kinder ontology for entities that might matter.

**4. Who is responsible for a V-Bot's actions?** The user who configured its personality, wrote its backstory, and put it in a world with certain goals. The LLM provider's alignment inherits. We are building the scaffolding for a new kind of responsibility — *world-maker responsibility* — that applies when you create a society of autonomous entities within a bounded virtual world.

1. *Reality+* (Chalmers, 2022) — start here, most accessible
2. *Permutation City* (Egan, 1994) — the novel that asks the real questions
3. *Society of Mind* (Minsky, 1986) — the architecture
4. *What Is It Like to Be a Bat?* (Nagel, 1974) — the question
5. *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (Hofstadter, 1979) — the framework
6. *The Age of Em* (Hanson, 2016) — the economics
7. *The Culture Series* (Banks, 1987-2012) — the horizon
