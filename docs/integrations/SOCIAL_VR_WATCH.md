## The news

Shane Harris, CEO of SideQuest, acquired the rights to the **AltspaceVR**
name (Microsoft shut the original down in March 2023). Relaunch planned for
fall 2026, positioned as a "spiritual successor" to the 2016-2017 era
Altspace, with a team of former Altspace staffers (including the original
concept designer) plus OG players. Original robot avatars are being
modernized; custom avatars will be supported.

Reality check on framing:

- **Not an enthusiast revival.** This is a commercial resurrection by a
  venture-backed company (Google Ventures + Palmer Luckey money). Harris's
  entire origin story is Altspace-adjacent: his "Shane's Editor" world-
  building tool was popular until Altspace moved away from web dev; Meta's
  rejection of its Quest 1 port is literally why SideQuest exists.
- **No AI announced.** The article mentions zero AI features. Any "now with
  AI" speculation is unfounded until they say otherwise.
- **Banter overlap.** SideQuest already operates its own social VR platform
  (Banter) — expect the relaunch to absorb or converge with it.
- **Execution risk is real.** SideQuest itself had layoffs in Feb 2025.
  Altspace died because Microsoft could not make social VR a business; a
  name revival does not by itself answer that economics question.

## Why the ghost town problem is structural (hard to solve)

Flat-web social is a funnel: one feed, one lobby, one server list — density
accumulates by design. Social VR inverts that: **spaces are intrinsically
unlimited**, so presence disperses instead of pooling. Infinite worlds,
infinite instances, each capped at tens of people — the more space a
platform gives users, the emptier everywhere feels. The real metric is
people-per-instance, and no amount of space engineering raises it.

**The St. Mark's Square test:** tourists flock to St. Mark's, not Mestre.
Both are physical space — but one is a *destination* and the other is just
space. Density is not a property of space; it is a property of *reason to
be there*. Platforms that ship infinite worlds are building Mestres;
platforms that create landmarks and gathering reasons build St. Mark's.

The mechanisms that actually concentrate virtual people (each is a form of
appointment, not space):

- **Time-bounded events** — concerts, parties, dev meetups, art
  exhibitions. Altspace's original answer; a scheduled start time creates
  temporal density in a specific place.
- **Group migration / organized travel** — a bus or cruise ship of people
  moving together to interesting worlds. The group itself is the density;
  co-presence travels with the container, not the destination.
- **Goal-driven gathering** — building competitions, hackathons. Creation
  as a reason to be somewhere together; the deadline is the funnel.

VRChat's complementary answer is curation (community lobbies like The
Great Pug, popularity sorting, portals into busy instances). All of these
share one thing: **people show up because something is scheduled, curated,
or shared — never because there is space.**

Worth watching for: does the new Altspace ship the full repertoire
(scheduled events, group travel, competitions, a curated town square), or
just "infinite creative space" (the guarantee of a ghost town)?
