# Vienna Makerspace Fleet Audit - Which Stack Do They Teach? (2026-10-18)

**Question from CLOSED_STACK_DISASTER.md:** if the makerspaces all run Bambus, our "try before you buy" advice teaches the wrong stack.

**Answer:** No - Vienna teaches the open stack. Happylab's FDM fleet is 100% Prusa. Metalab is RepRap by construction. TMW techLAB would not show us its models (login-gated page). Full breakdown below.

## 1. Happylab Wien - open FDM, closed resin (CONFIRMED from happylab.at/ausstattung)

FDM (the part that matters for our question):

* **6x Prusa i3 MK3S** - open hardware + open firmware, the classic workhorse
* **2x Prusa XL Multihead (multi-toolhead)** - open, and the single most relevant machine for Sandra: a real toolchanger to try multicolor BEFORE backing a QuarTeks single-nozzle bet
* Training: 3D-FDM Einschulung (Prusa), bookable via tickets site. Membership tiers Basic/Maker/Pro; FDM printers in the base machine park.

Also on site (flagged, not FDM):

* 2x Formlabs Form 3 + Wash + Cure - closed SLA ecosystem (resin locked to vendor cartridges, PreForm slicer). Fine for detail parts, teaches nothing about open FDM.
* Creality Raptor X - 3D SCANNER, not a printer. Do not cite as "Happylab runs Creality printers" - it does not, as far as the public page shows.
* Bonus for the filament question: Kunststoffverarbeitung corner with manual injection molder + **self-built PLA shredder**. That is the DIY version of the M1/R1 loop - shred and reuse scrap on site, no 899 EUR pledge needed. Try this before romanticizing home recycling.

Verdict: Happylab teaches exactly the right stack. Go there for: Prusa workflow basics on MK3S, multicolor reality check on XL multihead, scrap-shredding reality check for the M1 idea.

## 2. Metalab - RepRap roots (PARTIAL, wiki behind anti-bot wall)

* WhateverLab (WEL) lists 3D-Druck alongside laser, CNC, electronics. Wiki documents a **Mendel90** - nophead's Prusa-Mendel-derived RepRap, open down to the printed parts. Introduction via 3D Printer Workshop / core@metalab.at supervision.
* Caveat: metalab.at/wiki now sits behind Anubis proof-of-work anti-scrape, so the current fleet could not be verified remotely on 2026-10-18. The Mendel90 pages may be historical. Verify on site or via Matrix/IRC before citing a model list.
* By construction a hackerspace teaches open: whatever they run, it is member-maintained, reflashable, and documented in the wiki. The risk here is age (old machines, queue for induction), not closure.

## 3. TMW techLAB - free, models unverified (UNVERIFIED)

* Maker space inside Technisches Museum Wien, free drop-in format with Explainer support, plus roadLAB bus for schools. Online platform at techlab.tmw.at with project ideas and device infos.
* Problem: the Technologien device list renders behind a login wall (only auth chrome extracted 2026-10-18). No public model list found via search. Contact techlab@tmw.at or walk in (museum entry applies) to confirm what the 3D printers are.
* Best use: free first touch for orientation ("is 3D printing for me at all"), not stack evaluation.

## What this means for Sandra's plan

1. **Multicolor test before Oct 31:** book Happylab's 3D-FDM Einschulung, print one 3-4 color part on the Prusa XL multihead. That experience (purge tower size, filament change time, failure modes) is the baseline against which every i8 marketing number must be judged. Cost: membership + material, a fraction of a $309 pledge.
2. **Filament recycling test:** ask Happylab about the self-built shredder + injection molder corner. Shredding your own failed prints once teaches more about the M1/R1 workflow than ten campaign videos.
3. **Do not learn multicolor on a closed machine you do not own.** None of the three spaces offers Bambu/Creality multicolor to practice on - which is itself a data point about which stacks institutions trust.
4. **Re-check yearly:** spaces rotate fleets. If Happylab ever swaps Prusas for locked machines, that belongs in this file the same day - it would invert our try-before-you-buy advice.
